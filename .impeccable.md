# Briefly design direction

Read by the design skills (`frontend-design`, `critique`, `audit`) and the `ui-designer` agent. Token values live in `apps/web/src/index.css`; this file says why they exist and how to use them, never what they are.

## Design Context

### Users
Account managers, strategists and approvers at a campaign agency. They open Briefly between meetings to check whether a client brief is complete enough to start work, fix the AI's assessment where it is wrong, and pass it on for approval. They read carefully, work on desktop most of the day, and check status on a phone.

### Jobs
1. Pick the workspace (client account) they work in.
2. Paste or upload a brief and see it as a readable document.
3. Generate an AI review: which of the seven brief areas are present, unclear or missing, and what to ask the client.
4. Correct that review, then submit it for human approval.
5. As an approver: approve or request changes, with the reason visible to the submitter.

### Personality
A quiet brief desk with one rich moment. Calm, editorial and exact, like a well-kept document tool, not a dashboard. The brief itself (Newsreader) is the hero; the interface (Plex Sans) stays out of its way. The one rich moment is the review: the coverage strip and the present, unclear and missing colours. Everything else is quiet.

### Keep, unless a redesign is explicitly asked for
- Petrol teal means action or navigation and nothing else. Status hues appear only where they carry review meaning. One deliberate exception: Newsreader titles (brief titles, the sign-in headline) are set in `accent-hover`, the darkest teal, as ink on the brief desk; nothing else that is not interactive is teal.
- Brief status is told apart by dot shape first (open ring draft, heavy ring in review, filled approved or sent back), so it never rests on hue. Amber means "needs attention": an unclear area, or a brief sent back. The text always names which.
- Only theme tokens exist (Tailwind's palette is switched off). A new token needs the user's approval.
- Repeating controls use `.btn`, `.btn-primary`, `.btn-tonal`, `.btn-ghost`, `.input`, `.textarea`, `.card`, `.alert`, `.note`, `.meta`; one-offs are utilities in the markup.
- Fonts: Sans for interface, Mono for metadata and timestamps (`.meta`), Newsreader for brief titles and body.
- Radii from the scale (small, control, card, panel); one easing (`ease-out`), 150 ms for state changes.
- Confirmations are toasts through `notify()`; errors stay inline next to what caused them.

### Layout and type rules
- App shell: 248px sidebar from `md`, content capped at 1240px. At 400px the sidebar becomes a top bar.
- Brief text: `max-w-[68ch]`, 17px, `leading-7`. Keep reading text between 60 and 75 characters per line.
- Brief page: document left, status and actions in a sticky side column from `lg`; below `lg` the column stacks under the document.
- One primary action per view, and it is the next step in the workflow. Everything else is secondary, tonal or ghost.
- Every async view has loading, empty, error and success states; status that changes over time (generating, in review) says so in words, not only in colour.
- Targets at least 36px tall (`.btn` is `h-9`); icon-only buttons have an `aria-label`; focus is the global `:focus-visible` outline.

### Avoid
Gradients, glows, gradient text, decorative illustrations, emoji labels, uppercase tracked eyebrow labels, cards nested in cards, pulsing elements, colour used without meaning, and "dashboard" stat tiles. If a screen needs another card to make sense, the hierarchy is wrong.

## Reference log

Research findings from the `ui-designer` agent, kept so small fixes reuse them without new research. Each entry: date, source link, verified (seen on the page) or assumed, principle, what Briefly adopted, what it deliberately did not copy.

### 2026-11-18 — baseline review of the whole journey
- GitHub PR review — verified — https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/collaborating-with-pull-requests/reviewing-changes-in-pull-requests/reviewing-proposed-changes-in-a-pull-request. Edits stay pending until one deliberate submit; the decision form carries the reason. Adopted: open review edits hold back Submit; approve and request changes share one form. Not copied: a third "Comment" outcome, which has no effect in Briefly's workflow.
- Google Docs suggestions — verified — https://support.google.com/docs/answer/6033474. The decider sees what changed before accepting. Adopted: an "edited" `.meta` marker on areas the team changed. Not copied: coloured inline diffs in the brief, which would compete with the coverage colours.
- Vercel Geist Badge — verified — https://vercel.com/geist/badge. Status is short, one per row, readable without colour. Adopted: dot plus one or two words. Not copied: eight hue variants and pills.
- Linear workflow statuses — assumed (docs returned 404; search snippet and memory) — https://linear.app/docs/configuring-workflows. Lifecycle shown by icon shape. Adopted: hollow, ringed and filled status dots, so "In review" needs no teal. Not copied: custom statuses.
- NN/g visibility of system status (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visibility-system-status/), NN/g indicators (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications/), WCAG 1.4.1 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html) — verified. Status sits near what it describes, in words, never colour alone. Adopted: on mobile, status and the approver's reason come right after the header.
